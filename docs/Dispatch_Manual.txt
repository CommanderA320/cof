INTEGRATED OPERATIONS CONTROL DIRECTORATE  FOREWORD  As stated in the mission of the Incorporation, which has been operating in the field of air transport since 1933, the concepts of flight safety and security always come first and foremost among its core values. The principles determined within the scope of generating the most economical flight plan that will provide safe, secure and fuel saving in accordance with national and international regulations are included in the document under the following part. Part 1 - Organizational Structure Part 2 - Dispatch Operations Part 2.1 - Pre-Flight Dispatch Operations Part 2.2 - Dispatch Operations During Flight Planning Phase Part 2.3 - Flight Monitoring Process Part 3 – Trainings and Competencies Part 4 – Determination of Need for Dispatcher and Chief Controller Part 5 – Dispatch Operations Safety Management System  0. Management and Control  0.1. Purpose and Scope  In order to manage the flight operation safely, securely and economically, NOTAM, company notes and weather reports of the departure, arrival, alternate aerodromes and en-route airspaces used in the flight plan, evaluation of the weather conditions, generating of flight routes, obtaining permissions, performance restrictions and MEL/CDL records of the assigned aircraft. It is to define the procedures and principles for generating the most appropriate flight plan by providing control and following the flight from the beginning to the end of the flight. It covers the basic work activities, seniority ranking, promotion procedures and principles of Dispatchers and Chief Controllers as well the process of reporting the events and/or observations that may affect the flight safety negatively.  0.2. Aircraft Communications Addressing and Reporting System (ACARS):   Protocol allows the mutual transmission of required data/information between the aircraft and ground for the safe conduct of the flight.  Air Starter Unit (ASU):   The unit/machine that provides the air needed at the beginning of the aircraft engine starting process.  AIM:   Air Traffic Flow Management Information Message.  ANM:   Air Traffic Flow Management Notification Message.  AODB:   Airline Operational Database.  Airman's Meteorological Information (AIRMET):   Information issued by a meteorological watch office concerning the occurrence or expected occurrence of specified en-route weather phenomena which may affect the safety of low-level (FL150) aircraft operations.  ATC Slot [Calculated Take-Off Time (CTOT)]:   If any restriction is imposed on a region, an air corridor, or an airport, and it falls below the predetermined capacity, a regulation is published. CTOT is the take-off time NMOC allots, for the purpose of regulating the air traffic, to the flights that will take off or arrive within the time slot for which the regulation has been published.  ATIS:   Automatic Terminal Information Service.  Auxiliary Power Unit (APU):   Auxiliary power unit on board the aircraft.  Chief Controller:   Dispatcher ensure that documents for flight operations (flight plans and briefing packages) are prepared and controlled and monitor all phases for safe performance of flight operation in accordance with national and international rules and legislation of the Company.  Block Fuel:   The total quantity of fuel loaded to the aircraft.  B-RNAV:   Basic Area Navigation.  Chart:   Aeronautical chart.  Collaboration   Human   Machine   Interface   (CHMI):   It   is   a   standalone   application   which   provides   a   graphical   interface   for   the Eurocontrol Network Operations systems allowing users to display data and graphical information (such as routes, route attributes, airspaces, flight plan tracks, etc.) via map displays.  Contingency Fuel:   The fuel called contingency fuel and planned against the unexpected route and flight level deviations during the flight.  Cost Index:   The number entered into FMS to optimize the speed of the aircraft. It is the ratio between the unit time cost and the unit fuel cost.  Dispatch Safety Report (DSR):   The AQD report type that shall be completed by the dispatcher and the chief controller who work for the Company.  DME:   Distance Measuring Equipment.  EFB:   Electronic Flight Bag.  EEP:   ETOPS Enter Point.  EXP:   ETOPS Exit Point Flight Management System (FMS): Flight navigation computer in aircrafts.  Flight Plan Manager (FPM):   Flight Planning System.  Fuel Management Information System (FMIS):   The Fuel Management Information System is system in which the fuel consumption data are tracked on hourly, monthly, and annual basis by analyzing all flights performed by Turkish Airlines Inc against almost 1,000 parameters on the basis of planned flights and actual flight as broken down to aerodrome, aircraft, flight number, etc.  FMS NavData Service Provider:   Contracted FMS Navigation Data supplier firm.  Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS):   Geo-positioning system with global coverage via satellites.  GPS:   Global Positining System  High Frequency (HF):   The radio frequency band with a bandwidth of 3,000 to 30,000 kHz, used to communicate with the aircraft.  ILS:   Instrument Landing System. Network Management Operation Center (NMOC):   The Eurocontrol department that regulates the traffic capacity in the European airspace.  OpsEye:   Türkiye's first local integrated Flight Monitoring system developed by Turkish Airlines. It is the first flight monitoring system produced in Türkiye, which is self-dependent and obtains its own flight data from its own sources. Organized Track System (OTS):   Routes published on daily basis and dependent on the wind and weather conditions in case of ocean passing. Receiver Autonomous Integrity Monitoring (RAIM):   It is technology developed to evaluate the integrity of GPS signals.  RCP240:   Required Communication Performance – 240 seconds.  Reduced Contingency Fuel (RCF) Planning / Re-clear:   Planning with reduced contingency fuel.  RNAV:   Area Navigation.  RSP180:   Required Surveillance Performance – 180 seconds.  SATCOM:   Satellite Communications.  Selective Calling Radio System (SELCAL):   The radio system that warns the cockpit crew on board the aircraft, either in the air or on the ground, when the ground station wants to establish contact with them. Even if the radio of the cockpit crew is in the mute mode, SELCAL warning may warn the cockpit crew for any incoming radio signal.  Short Flight Plan (SFP):   Dispatcher Release Form. SFP that is first page of the flight plan is added to briefing package as a separate document.  Significant Meteorological Information (SIGMET):   The meteorological report through which the possible enroute significant weather events are reported.  Special Weather Report (SPECI):   The instant meteorological publication made when worsening of meteorological parameters is observed in the published observation data between two METAR publications. FPM (Flight Planning System)  For the flights that do not require overflight permissions from the countries, it generates optimized routes by considering the fuel, overflight,   crew,   aircraft   maintenance   costs,   and   compares   the   costs   of   all   routes   by   simulating   the   routes   it   has   generated simultaneously with the company routes. For the flights that require special overflight permissions, on the other hand, it is not possible to generate optimized routes; the flight plans can be generated only with using the company routes for which the overflight permissions have already been obtained. The flight planning system simulates and compares the generated company routes with each other on the basis of fuel, overflight, crew, and maintenance costs, and selects the route with the lowest total cost automatically. The flight planning system determines a Cost Index for each flight by evaluating all data. The flight plans are generated by using numerous data coming to the flight planning system through the Company systems. Weather Conditions Information:   It comes from DTN.  NOTAM Information:   It comes from the European AIS Database (EAD).  Navigation Data:   It comes from LIDO. Dispatch Operations  The Dispatch Operations generally consist of the pre-flight, flight planning, and flight monitoring processes.  2.1. Pre-Flight Dispatch Operations 2.1.1. Obtaining Permission Process (Landing/Take-off & Overflight)  Integrated Operation Control Directorate/Operation Planning and Support Directorate provides overflight permits for all scheduled flights (cargo/passenger), charters, VIP charters and individual additional cargo flights planned within the operation time frame. The landing permission are obtained by the departments that have planned the flight and/or the relevant stations. In the obtaining landing permission process, for several countries (such as Russia, Belarus, India) that request special form, Integrated Operations Control Directorate fills out and sends that form to the authority or station officials. The dispatcher generates a flight plan for each flight by using the existing route if available (If not, a new route is generated). The dispatcher/assigned employees apply for overflight permission in compliance with the relevant country’s civil aviation regulations on the basis of the information on the airspace used (A form is filled in, a telex is sent, an e-mail is sent, or the application is made through the online websites). For VIP charter and cargo flights that require diplomatic permission (ammunition, military equipment, etc.), the general permission form and the required country forms are filled out and sent to the relevant unit of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Turkey via e-mail in order to obtain permission from the country authorities. For the permission for which the relevant application has been made, the necessary coordination is established with the station and the relevant authority. The number of the obtained permission is recorded in the flight planning system. It is checked regularly that all passenger charter and cargo flights to be performed within the operational time frame have permissions. For the flights without permissions and/or the permission of which have expired, obtaining permission process is commenced. The overflight permissions are monitored and recorded as specified in the requirements in   EK.10.74.006 Operations Management  Manual Chapter 6. Corporate Operation Memory.  2.1.2.
